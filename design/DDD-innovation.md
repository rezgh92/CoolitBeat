# DDD-innovation: innovation register, PRISM-15 Rev A

> Lane `innovation`, 2026-10-16. Parent: `docs/cooling/03-architecture.md` (0.1-0.2, 2.1-2.3, 3.1-3.5, 4.1-4.3, 5.1-5.3, 7.2, 9). Inputs: every DDD lane (`DDD-core`, `DDD-grate`, `DDD-plenum`, `DDD-mech`, `DDD-mfg`, `DDD-perf`) and the verification reports under `appendix/verify/`. This is an engineering register for the design report: every benefit is a modelled P50 number (±25 % until E1-E4) with the script it comes from; every novelty statement carries the FTO caveat below. `params-innovation.json` is a stub index of the features.

**FTO caveat (applies to every row).** `appendix/verify/verify-ip.md` is engineering-level patent reading from snippets (about 40 searches, no full claim set read, WebFetch blocked). It is not legal advice. Its findings that bind this register: (a) the kerf is a *weak* design-around of the CoolIT Split-Flow 2008 family ('330/'284/'906/'513, live to about Aug-Sep 2028, '330 PTA bound about Jun 2031); about 49 % of a segment's inflow still enters through fin tips under the 1.2 mm window (`ip_entry_split.py`), and the kerf is worth 2.6 % of the cell dT, so the DOE reading stays open; (b) Microfabrica US 12,255,123 ("hybrid microjet and microchannel array") is medium-high risk unless the jets land on the lip and not on the fins, which Rev A does; (c) Intel 12,133,357 / 11,804,418 and Toyota 11,428,478 are live, close and uncharted; (d) TW I606224 is live to about 2032 for Taiwan manufacture; (e) of the five draft filings only #1 (narrow combination with numeric limits) and #5 (defensive publication of the TTV method) are worth filing; #2 and #4 are anticipated or obvious; #3 is a trade secret. No US offers, sales or evaluation units before the outside-counsel opinion (US$150-250k, weeks 2-8); US launch planned on or after 10 Sep 2028. "Novelty confidence" below is therefore the engineering team's estimate of what survives a search, not an opinion on infringement. Filings and marketing avoid "split", "between the ends" and "halves the path".

Confidence scale: **high** = no close structural match found and the combination carries numeric limits; **medium** = elements known, combination or limits possibly new; **low** = known practice, publish defensively; **none** = standard practice, listed in section 3.

### 1.1 Short, flux-graded flow length (L 7.7 mm on the TTV; 5-12 mm by zone flux)

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Problem | A 54 mm split-flow path carries the caloric rise and the fin-top short-circuit along the fins: CoolIT's measured 34.4 / 44.2 K at 15 kW includes a reconstructed base term of 6.6 K and a maximum-minus-average of 9.8 K. Long paths also force coarse gaps for pressure drop |
| Mechanism | 15 lines at 7.7143 mm pitch (7 inlet, 8 outlet, edge outlets with half windows) give 5,040 parallel 7.4 mm fin segments at 0.20 m/s, Re 44. Fin dP is 1.07 kPa, so the gap (0.15 mm) is set by fouling and h_eff (117 kW/m2K), not by dP. Flow length is graded by zone flux: ≥ 250 W/cm2 5-6 mm, 100-250 W/cm2 6-8 mm, 50-100 W/cm2 8-12 mm, < 50 W/cm2 12 mm (architecture 3.3, corrected: L 4 mm is never best; the ripple crest is flat within 0.35 K over L 5-8 mm) |
| Quantified benefit | Benchmark 31.1 / 38.8 K paste, 29.2 / 34.9 K PCM at base 0.85 (`perf_datasheet.out` §1; architecture 31.0 / 38.7 and 29.0 / 34.8 at 0.80, `final_thermal.out` §1). Capacity at Tcase ≤ 85 C: 15.7 / 18.4 kW vs ~12.9 / 14.8 kW (+22-24 %). Low-flow concession: the average is lost by 17 % at 0.25 LPM/kW (isothermal-cell caloric penalty, `perf_datasheet.out` §4) |

### 1.2 Kerfed skived fins with full-height entry

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Problem | Fluid entering a fin field from above through a window runs along the fin tips (Hele-Shaw short-circuit) and leaves the fin roots, where the heat is, under-fed; the fins are unmixed across their height at Re 35-200 |
| Mechanism | 13 interior kerfs 0.30 +0.05/-0 wide, sawn 1.925 ± 0.025 deep from the planed tip plane so the floor sits 0.05-0.10 above the fin root (never into the base; hinge ≥ 0.80), full depth over \|y\| ≤ 48.1 with an arc ramp to zero at 54.0 so the saw never touches the rim; 2 edge channels 0.35 wide (DDD-core 5). Kerf permeability w²/12 is ~8x the fin-gap value, so the kerf acts as a distribution duct feeding the segment end face over the full 2.0 mm height; kerf top ≤ 1.3 m/s, fin-gap 0.198 m/s. 15 micro-grain carbide slitting saws Ø20 x 0.28 on one arbor, wax-potted fins, tooth force 1.3-2.1 N vs 1.4-5 N fin lateral capacity, 10,080 cut sites deburred to ≤ 15 µm |
| Quantified benefit | Kerf worth 0.4 K on Rev A and 0.66 K on the draft core (architecture 3.2; `vp_cell.py` / `vp_run1.py` confirm 0.66 K at L 6: no-kerf 23.54 K); +0.30 K if the inlet kerf fills by impaction, +0.66 K if both fill (`verify-physics.md` §2c); kerf permeability ratio 4-16 moves the result ±0.25 K. Arc ramp costs +0.014 K average (`core_geom.out` §3). Cost US$2.8 at 100k (`mfg_cost.out` §3); dropping the kerfs saves US$3.4 and costs +0.4-0.7 K (`verify-close-the-losses.md`) |
| Closest prior art | Cross-cut skived fins (established practice, verify-ip #1 row); Toyota US 11,428,478 (gridded fins in an MMC sink, live to ~2040); Intel 11,804,418; Cooligy US 6,986,382 interdigitated fingers; LLNL Bonde US 5,099,311 and Walpole US 5,099,910 (expired). **Difference:** the kerf is a sub-0.3 mm saw cut that stops 0.05-0.10 above the base in a tied field, sized so its top-entry velocity stays ≤ 1.3 m/s and its area (1.33x the orifice area) does not meter; the slit is a *distribution duct*, not the fin entry. CoolIT '330 claims an inlet opening "between the ends" of a continuous channel; verify-ip finds the kerf does not end the flow path |
| Novelty confidence | **Low-medium.** Novel only as the narrow combination of filing #1 (kerf/window ratio, floor stub, orifice-to-braze clearance ≥ 0.35 mm, meter/header head ratio ≥ 6); detectable by teardown, easy to design around. The stronger structural fix (window narrowed to the kerf, -0.11 K, `cell2d.py`) is carried as the pre-freeze option; the no-kerf R1 fallback is non-US only (caveat (a)) |
| Proving test | E1 skive and gang-saw trial: pitch and thickness Cpk ≥ 1.33, burr ≤ 15 µm by profilometer on 3 kerfs x 5 fins per lot, root metallography, ISO 16232 flush with no particle > 50 µm. E2 single-cell CFD + water-table PIV: kerf share of segment inflow, fin-top velocity ≤ 0.5 m/s at 18 LPM. Fin-field dP taps on the first five plates: 1.07 ± 0.2 kPa viscous term (> 1.3 = filler in kerfs or windows) |

### 1.3 Off-braze-plane graded orifice meter in the grate web, jets landing on the grate lip

|---|---|

| Mechanism | Two OFE laminations brazed in the one cycle: land plate GRT-L 1.30 (windows 1.2 x 108, recess pockets 2.70 wide x 0.80 deep from the top face, capillary-break grooves 0.15 x 0.08) and meter web GRT-W 0.40 at z 4.10-4.50 with 378 inlet orifices 0.50 x 0.99-1.12 (r 0.10) in 5 size steps spanning 1.134x in area, centred at x_line ± 1.00, alternating sides so each 0.75 lip receives 27 jets per line. Stand-off H 0.80 = 1.6 orifice widths (inside the potential core), jet footprint 0.670-1.330 on the 0.60-1.35 lip, inner margin 0.070 nominal / 0.023 RSS. Orifices ≥ 0.40 from the nearest filler (rule ≥ 0.35); the 0.3 mm mask is a starve zone and the grooves stop the capillary front. Because the plenum profile and the orifice loss both scale with u², one artwork balances every flow |
| Quantified benefit | Per-orifice max/min ±2.2 % at 9, 18, 27 and 36 LPM vs 1.118-1.122 ungraded (`grate_artwork.out`; target ±3 %); robust to Cd 0.65-0.80 (1.044-1.055), a ±2 mm window move and a two-arch feed (1.058). Meter 2.31 kPa at 1.54 m/s mean, 1.64 m/s max orifice, 2.0 m/s cap reached at 23.4 LPM (`grate_cell.out`, `perf_datasheet.out` §8). Fin-top estimate 0.40 m/s at 18 LPM vs the 0.5 cap (DDD-grate 5). | Closest prior art | Cooligy US 6,986,382 / 7,156,159 (interdigitated manifold over microchannels, expired); Mikros normal-flow (NCP) laminated plates where jets through thin sheets strike the heated base; JetCool / MIT US 11,018,077 and 11,594,470 (microjets onto the package with local returns); Microfabrica US 12,255,123 (hybrid microjet + microchannel, to ~2042, parent 9,953,899 to ~2036); IBM Zurich hierarchical manifold with direct jet impingement; orifice-compensated headers in plate HX practice. **Difference:** the jets never touch the heat-exchange surface: they stagnate on a dedicated lip of the grate 1.3 mm above the fin tips and the fluid then descends a 1.2 mm window at ≤ 0.4 m/s; the meter sits 0.35-0.40 mm clear of any braze plane; the grading is a 5-step quantised area ladder solved on a 432-cell 1-D manifold model and re-issued per package |

### 1.4 Tied sandwich with a 0.85 mm pressure-neutral base

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Problem | In a conventional plate the base is a pressure vessel wall: at MAWP 1.03 MPa the 117 cm2 field carries 12 kN, so the base is 2-3 mm thick (CoolIT reconstruction base term 6.6 K) and bows under pressure and clamp, which opens PCM and LM bond lines |
| Mechanism | Fin tips (planed coplanar with the rim top within 0.02) are brazed to the grate lands with 12-15 µm BAg-8 preform ladders; the grate carries the plenum legs, the separator and the cap feet, so the pressure load on the base is self-equilibrated by 360 fins x 14 segments of ties and never enters the clamp budget (DDD-mech 5). The base can then be fly-cut after braze to 0.85 (+0.05/-0.05; 0.80-0.90 accepted; 1.0 fallback) at ≤ 20 µm flatness, Ra ≤ 0.8. The governing defect is one unbonded land strip spanning kerf to kerf; the 0.85 base is gated on E1 showing per-strip detection by the confocal proof scan (bulge ~1.8 µm at 1.55 MPa) |
| Quantified benefit | Base term 2.79 K at 0.85 vs 6.6 K in the CoolIT calibration budget and 3.29 K at the 1.0 fallback (+0.55 K average, +0.36 K max; `perf_datasheet.out` §1). Tie stress 8.9-11.6 MPa mean, 22-28 MPa local vs 40 MPa allowable at 3.1 MPa design burst; plenum wall 29.8, separator 22.8 MPa (`ctl/pkgA_dp_stress.out`, DDD-grate 8). Clamp bow 22.6 µm concave at 2 kN TTV vs thermal +18.7 µm convex at 15 kW → net -4.0 µm (`mech_calc.out` §7). Fly-cut on the tied field: foundation modulus ~29 kN/mm3, 0.3 µm under 50 N thrust, no chatter (verify-mfg #13). Unverified upside: a flat tied face reduces the mean paste BLT, -0.2 to -0.5 K per µm (`verify-close-the-losses.md` 2.5) |
| Closest prior art | Brazed plate-fin heat exchangers (fins bonded to both parting sheets, standard since the 1940s); vacuum-brazed cold plates with a lid brazed onto skived or folded fin tips (common); CoolIT compliant-member and IBM TCM clamping for flatness. **Difference:** the tie is used deliberately to take the pressure boundary out of the heat path (base thinner than any fin-root hinge rule would allow for a vessel wall) and is controlled per land strip, with the defect case (one strip, collapse 1.8-2.6 MPa, leak-tight at MAWP) analysed and screened |
| Novelty confidence | **Low** for the tie itself; **medium** for the thin-base rating logic and the per-strip screen, which is a trade secret (filing #3: unenforceable offshore and patenting would disclose it). Both are at parity with the proven-subset positioning |
| Proving test | E1 tie-braze matrix: every land strip ≥ 70 % bonded over any 10 mm (CT), unbonded-strip bulge detected at base 0.85 by confocal at 1.55 MPa, burst ≥ 4.5 MPa, no plastic set after lot over-proof 2.5-3.1 MPa, flatness ≤ 20 µm after fly-cut. E3 heated pressurised flatness scan (O-M2) separates the 22.6 and 18.7 µm terms |

### 1.7 Fin-field dP taps as the fouling and build signature

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Problem | Port-to-port dP is 81 % inertial (meter, ports, headers): a 10 µm deposit on each fin wall raises fin dP 54 % but plate dP only 6-8 %, so no port measurement can see fouling or a bad tie braze (`verify-hydraulics.md` §4) |
| Mechanism | Two Ø1.00 gun-drilled or EDM taps along y at z 1.40 from -y bosses at x 0.000 (inlet line k = 7) and x +15.429 (outlet line k = 9), 15.35 long, with Ø1.65 x 4.0 counterbores for Cu 1.6 x 0.3 stubs brazed in the same cycle (DDD-core 3.10; -T SKUs). They read the fin-field pressure difference (inlet kerf to outlet kerf): the 1.07 kPa viscous term, linear in flow and viscosity |
| Quantified benefit | Fouling sensitivity x7-9 over port taps; thresholds +10 % warning (≈ 2 µm per wall), +25 % service (≈ 5 µm) (architecture 7.2). Production use: > 1.3 kPa = filler in kerfs or windows, < 0.9 kPa = missed tip joints (bypass) (`perf_datasheet.md` §9 item 4). Converts the unmeasurable 10-year life into a dose qualification: 100x particle dose for 1,000 h (~11 years) with ≤ 10 % fin-field dP drift and ≤ 1 K (E4). Cost in the TAP/GUARD line US$8.3 total |
| Closest prior art | Heat-exchanger fouling monitoring by dP (universal); filter dP alarms; CoolIT US 9,052,252 (in-plate leak sensing); NVIDIA "intelligent plate" filings (uncharted). **Difference:** the taps straddle only the viscous element inside a manifold plate, which is possible because the inlet and outlet plenums are physically separate layers above the field; sensor records published as PLDM/Redfish, plate stays passive |
| Novelty confidence | **Low-medium.** Possibly new as a cold-plate feature; of little value as a patent (infringed by the operator, detectable only in a fleet). Publish with the OCP method; chart CoolIT '252 and the NVIDIA filings before any tray-module design |
| Proving test | First five plates: fin-field dP at 6 flows against the 1.07 kPa model; E4 dose test with the taps as the primary metric; correlation of tap reading with CT-confirmed tie defects on E1 coupons |

### 1.10 Filtration ladder

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Problem | A 0.15 mm gap behind a 50 µm absolute filter has ~7 years of exclusion life (a 0.2 mm gap ~15); the drop-in plate cannot win fouling life at equal filtration and must not sieve anywhere upstream of the fins |
| Mechanism | Passage hierarchy port 20 > header 8 > plenum 5 > window 1.2 > orifice 0.5 > kerf 0.3 > gap 0.15 mm, every stage ≥ 3x the last filter's absolute rating. Ladder: supplied tray cartridge ≥ 150 cm2 pleated 33 µm absolute 316L with dP taps and dry-break valves → plate guard 50 µm absolute square-weave 316L as an inside-out radial diffuser in the inlet boss (≥ 25 cm2, 0.12 m/s face) → fins. ## 2. Design story (one page, plain engineering)

The incumbent plate solves a 15 kW problem the way every cold plate has for twenty years: a thick copper base as the pressure vessel wall, fins on top, and a lid. Coolant comes in one end of the fin field and runs tens of millimetres along the fins. That long path does three bad things at once. The coolant warms as it goes, so the far end runs hotter; the pressure drop of a long thin channel forces the designer to coarsen the gaps, which cuts the heat-transfer coefficient; and because the fluid enters from above and the fins are unmixed across their height, a good share of it runs along the fin tips and never reaches the roots where the heat is. The base, being a vessel wall, is 2-3 mm thick and adds several kelvin of its own.

PRISM takes those four problems apart one at a time with ordinary processes. First, it cuts the path to 7.7 mm by feeding and draining the fin field from above at fifteen lines: 5,040 short segments in parallel instead of a few hundred long ones. The fin dP falls to about 1 kPa, so the gap can be the 0.15 mm that heat transfer and filtration want rather than what pressure drop allows. Second, under every feed and drain line a 0.3 mm saw kerf lets the coolant enter the segment over the full 2 mm fin height, which closes the tip short-circuit and is worth about half a kelvin. Third, the fins are brazed at their tips to a grate above them, so the fin field itself carries the pressure load as thousands of small ties. The base stops being a vessel wall and is fly-cut after braze to 0.85 mm, flat to 20 µm; it no longer bows under pressure or clamp, which is what lets PCM and liquid metal hold their datasheet resistance. Fourth, the grate is also the meter: graded orifices in a thin web above a stepped recess set each line's flow so that it is uniform along the plenum at every flow rate, because the plenum's pressure profile and the orifice loss both scale with velocity squared. The jets strike a lip of the grate, not the fins, and the fluid then descends a wide window at low speed.

Everything above the grate is sheet metal: a plenum folded from one strip, a separator, and a cap drawn with its own skirt that laps onto the base land, so the plate has one external seam plus two port bosses and every internal joint can only bypass, never leak. The whole stack is brazed in one vacuum cycle at 0.08 MPa fixture pressure. The predicted result at the published 15 kW, 18 LPM point is 31.1 / 38.8 K on paste against the measured 34.4 / 44.2 K, 29.2 / 34.9 K on a PCM pad, at 7.6 kPa plate pressure drop, 20.8 mm at the bar and 1.24-1.28 kg, for about US$165 at 100k per year. The honest concessions are that it costs about 31 % more per plate (parity per kW), has a shorter fouling exclusion life at equal filtration and therefore ships with a 33 µm tray cartridge, loses the average at very low flow, and has no field record. None of the parts is new. What is new is the way four known ideas are made to hold each other up in one brazed stack built from catalogue processes, with the numbers that make them fit.

## 4. Stretch innovations (not in Rev A)

**S1. Staggered (chevron) line pattern to halve the kerf-to-kerf ripple.** The maximum carries half the computed case ripple, 5.39 K pk-pk at base 0.85, because every fin gap sees the same inlet-outlet sequence along x and the base can only smooth it over the 3.7 mm half-pitch by lateral conduction. If adjacent lines were offset by half a segment along y (a chevron or stepped kerf and matching chevron plenum channels), the hot end of one cell would face the cold end of its neighbour 0.3 mm away across a fin, and the conduction length that smooths the ripple would fall from the 3.7 mm half-pitch to about half the stagger period. Conduction smoothing scales roughly with length squared, so a 4 mm stagger period could cut the ripple by a factor of two or more, worth about -1.3 K on the maximum and nothing on the average. Caveat: gang saws cut straight lines, so the kerfs would need wire-EDM or laser (the kerf is 2.8 US$ today, perhaps 8-15 as EDM), the plenum fold tool becomes a chevron tool, the along-plenum artwork solver becomes 2-D, and a transverse slot pattern over parallel channels reads closer to the Cooligy and Split-Flow art, so it would need its own chart.

**S2. Viscosity-driven self-balancing of hot segments.** Fin dP is viscous (∝ µ u) and the PG25 film viscosity falls about 30 % between 35 and 50 C (1.60 → 1.12 mPa·s). At fixed segment pressure, a segment under a hotter zone would therefore draw more flow in proportion to its own temperature rise, a passive negative feedback that an inertial meter deliberately suppresses (the meter was chosen so the balance is flow-independent). If a zoned SKU moved part of the 2.3 kPa inertial meter budget into a viscous element (a longer or thinner outlet path, or a laminar-flow row of 0.2 mm slots at Re < 100), a 10 K hotter segment would self-feed by 10-15 % with no moving parts. Caveat: the plenum profile sensitivity that the u² meter cancels comes back in proportion to the viscous share; the gain is confined to zones with ≥ 10 K local coolant rise; the 0.2 mm viscous element breaks the "gap ≥ 3x filter" ladder unless it sits downstream of the fins; and water loops (µ 0.55 mPa·s, weaker temperature slope) get less than half the effect. Worth a `cell2d`/`plenum_graded` study, not a build.

**S3. 0.60 mm base with 100 % per-strip tie verification by lock-in thermography.** The base term is 3.29 K per mm (2.79 K at 0.85), so a 0.60 base would save about 0.8 K on every TIM and 0.5 K on the maximum, and it is still 4x the 0.15 fin thickness for the fly-cut. The pressure-neutral tie makes it structurally possible: wall and tie stresses do not change with base thickness. What changes is the governing defect: one unbonded land strip collapses at 1.8-2.6 MPa at 0.85 and, scaling with t², at about 0.9-1.3 MPa at 0.60, which is at or below MAWP 1.03 MPa, so it stops being a rating problem and becomes a safety problem that the confocal proof scan alone (resolution 1 µm, bulge ~1.8 µm at 0.85) cannot be trusted to catch at 100 %. The enabling step is a second independent screen: lock-in or pulsed thermography on the as-brazed face before fly-cut, where an unbonded strip 7.4 x 1.8 mm shows as a phase lag of the order of the fin-tip joint's contact resistance over a 0.6-0.85 mm copper skin (thermal diffusion time L²/α ≈ 9 ms for 1 mm Cu at α 1.1e-4 m2/s, so a 5-30 Hz lock-in band). Caveat: the method is unproven on a 50 %-solid fin foundation that itself conducts heat sideways; it would need an E1 sub-study with deliberately unbonded strips, and the filing route is trade secret (filing #3 logic), so it protects only our own plates.

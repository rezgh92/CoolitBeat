Manufacturing

One furnace cycle, sixteen critical characteristics, every plate tested

1

Skive the core from a 4.3 mm OFE blank

2

Pot, gang-saw 15 kerfs, plane the tip plane to ±20 µm

3

Mill and groove the land plate; laser and electropolish the web

4

Fold the plenum, laser the separator, draw the cap

5

Stack on 2 pins with etched BAg-8 foils; vacuum braze 795 °C, 60 per load

6

Helium leak test dry; infrared flow screen

7

Fly-cut the die face to 0.85 mm with in-line ultrasonic gauge

8

Flush, proof with bulge scan, dP at 3 flows, bake, N₂ pack

Braze fixture FIX-15

Graphite platens, four TZM tie rods and eight TZM leaf springs turn the copper stack’s extra 0.137 mm of thermal growth into 1,250 N at 795 °C: 0.08 MPa on the stack, insensitive to shim error. 9.5 kg per fixture, 60 per furnace load, 300 cycles of life. Dead weight would have been 128 kg per plate.

Inspection that finds the real failures

100 % on every plate: fin thickness and pitch by vision, tip plane by laser scan, all 756 orifices by backlit vision, base thickness by ultrasonics, helium leak at 10⁻⁶ mbar·L/s, cleanliness to ISO 16232, proof with a 1 µm confocal bulge map, dP within ±10 %, infrared flow screen. By lot: CT of 1 in 200, sections of 1 in 1,000, burst 1 in 2,000.

Yield 93.5 % mature, 80 % at the 10k per year ramp. Second sources for skiving and brazing qualified by month 18. Build site outside Taiwan at a non-Group-A contract manufacturer (Vietnam or Mexico).

The routing is ordered so that helium testing happens before any wet step and the infrared screen before the fly-cut coats the die face. The fixture is the one piece of real process invention in the manufacturing lane: a differential-expansion clamp that needs no weights and gives a load the furnace temperature itself regulates.

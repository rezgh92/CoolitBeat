Layers 3 to 5 · Plenum, separator, cap

Folded, stamped and drawn: no forging, no machined manifold

Plenum: one 0.8 mm sheet

Folded into 16 walls and 15 channels of 6.9 × 5.0 mm at 7.7143 mm pitch, held by two slotted end frames. Inlet channels take coolant through ceiling windows; 14 bleed holes leave no stagnant corner. Velocity at or below 0.65 m/s.

Separator: 1.8 mm plate

14 inlet windows at y −21 and −7, 32 outlet windows at +7, +21, +35 and +49; 0.46 kPa. A 0.6 mm 316L option saves 106 g.

Cap: drawn 1.0 mm, skirted

Two inlet and four outlet arches, 10 × 7.7 mm internal at 14 mm pitch (the widest a 1.0 mm sheet can form with a brazeable valley), 35 mm cross domes under the 20 mm bosses, and a skirt drawn to 121.5 / 123.4 that laps 1.3 mm onto the rim. That lap, with one silver ring, is the plate’s only external seam. Bar height 20.8 mm.

Cap lane complete: arches drawn before the skirt, anneal, restrike; 279 g. Open: dome-wall FEA (28 to 48 MPa bound), the soft-tool draw trial, and the in-dome 17 cm² guard versus a serviceable 25 cm² stub housing.

The verification round found that stamped laminations could not be made at these web widths and that a forged header bar cost height, mass and money the physics did not need. The folded plenum and the drawn cap replaced them and took the plate from 26.4 to about 21 mm and from 1.74 to about 1.2 kg.
